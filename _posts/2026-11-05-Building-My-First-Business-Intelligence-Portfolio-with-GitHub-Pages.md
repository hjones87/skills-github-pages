title: "Building My First Business Intelligence Portfolio with GitHub Pages"
date: 2026-11-05

As a Business Intelligence professional, I've spent the last few years creating dashboards, developing reporting solutions and helping organisations turn data into meaningful insights. However, much of my work has been spread across different platforms, including Tableau Public, GitHub repositories and project documentation. I wanted a place where I could bring everything together and showcase my work in a more professional and accessible way.

That's what inspired me to create my first GitHub Pages portfolio.

## Why Create a Portfolio Website?

When applying for data and BI roles, a CV can only tell part of the story. It's useful for highlighting experience and achievements, but it doesn't always provide an opportunity to demonstrate technical skills, visualisations or problem-solving approaches.

A portfolio website allows me to showcase projects in more detail, giving visitors the chance to explore not only what I've built, but also the thinking behind it. Whether it's a Tableau dashboard, a Power BI report or a SQL-based analytics project, I want to highlight the business problems being solved rather than simply displaying screenshots.

GitHub Pages felt like the ideal solution because it's free, easy to manage and integrates naturally with GitHub projects.

## Planning the Website

Before writing any code, I spent some time thinking about the purpose of the website and the audience I wanted to reach.

My primary goal was to create a portfolio that would be useful for recruiters, hiring managers and fellow data professionals. With that in mind, I've structured the site around a few key areas:

- A brief introduction and professional profile
- Featured Power BI and Tableau projects
- GitHub repositories and technical work
- Skills, certifications and achievements
- Blog content and project write-ups
- Contact information and professional links

Rather than creating a simple gallery of dashboards, I want each project to tell a story.

## Focusing on Business Impact

One thing I've learned throughout my career is that visualisations are only part of the solution. The real value comes from understanding the problem, analysing the data and delivering insights that support decision-making.

For that reason, each portfolio project will include:

- The business challenge
- The tools and technologies used
- The approach taken
- Key findings and insights
- Outcomes and impact

My hope is that this demonstrates analytical thinking and business understanding, not just technical capability.

## A New Learning Experience

Although I regularly work with BI tools, building a website is a new challenge for me. Creating a GitHub Pages site provides an opportunity to develop additional skills in areas such as web development, version control and content creation.

It's also a reminder that learning doesn't stop once you've mastered a particular tool. Technology continues to evolve, and the best professionals are those who remain curious and willing to explore something new.

## Looking Ahead

This portfolio is very much a work in progress, but that's part of the journey. Over time, I'll be adding Tableau visualisations, Power BI case studies, technical projects and blog posts covering topics that interest me within the world of data and analytics.

My goal is simple: to create a space that showcases my work, shares my experiences and documents my continued growth as a Business Intelligence professional.

And this first blog post feels like the perfect place to start.
